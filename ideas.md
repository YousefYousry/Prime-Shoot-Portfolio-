# Prime Shot Creative — Design Direction

## Approach 1
**Theme Name:** Olive Noir Editorial

**Very Brief Intro:** A cinematic studio archive built from near-black space, olive accents, and image-led editorial composition. It treats the portfolio as a living contact sheet: quiet, confident, and built for close looking.

**Probability:** 0.04

## Approach 2

## Selected Approach: Olive Noir Editorial

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial minimalism with references to independent art-direction studios, fashion lookbooks, and cinematic film title sequences.

### Core Principles
1. Let the work lead: imagery gets scale, while interface chrome stays restrained.
2. Use tension between rigid metadata and expressive image crops to create an art-directed rhythm.
3. Keep the palette disciplined: black and warm off-white hold the frame; olive is a signal, never wallpaper.
4. Make every interaction feel like turning a page in a well-made visual archive.

### Color Philosophy
The near-black ground gives photography room to carry the color and emotion. Warm white keeps typography calm rather than stark, while a muted olive green marks moments of intent—active filters, arrows, and the studio’s signature cursor-like details. The palette should feel tactile and materially grounded, not digitally glossy.

### Layout Paradigm
A vertical editorial sequence with offset modules, asymmetric image pairings, and long horizontal separators. The page should feel composed rather than tiled: large images establish rhythm, while slim metadata rails and small labels create the feeling of a cataloging system.

### Signature Elements
- Olive index numbers and hairline rules that annotate each project like a contact sheet.
- A small rotating “PS” stamp used sparingly as a section marker and footer motif.
- Project hover states that reveal an olive-tinted veil, title, category, year, and diagonal arrow.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions are quiet and useful. Hovering reveals context without obscuring the image; filtering rearranges the archive instantly; mobile navigation opens as a full-page index. Controls feel editorial—more like labels in a gallery than buttons in an application.

### Animation
Use short opacity and transform transitions with a strong ease-out. Hero elements enter in staggered, slightly offset bands. Project images lift by a few pixels and scale minimally on hover. The intro mark uses a brief line-draw/reveal treatment, never a long loading screen. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep all routine interactions below 300ms.

### Typography System
Use **Space Grotesk** for display headlines and **DM Mono** for metadata, labels, and navigation. Display text is uppercase, tightly tracked, and oversized; metadata is small, monospaced, and olive or warm gray. Body copy uses Space Grotesk at a comfortable reading size with generous line height.

### Brand Essence
Prime Shot Creative is a visual production studio for brands that want sharper, more memorable stories—combining photography, creative production, and graphic design in one art-directed practice. Personality: **cinematic, exacting, quietly bold**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and declarative. CTAs feel like invitations into the archive, not sales language. Microcopy is precise, observant, and never padded with agency clichés.

Example lines:
- “WE CREATE VISUAL STORIES FOR BRANDS.”
- “LOOK CLOSER / ENTER THE ARCHIVE”

### Wordmark & Logo
Keep the supplied geometric “P” symbol as the primary brand mark. Pair it with a custom stacked wordmark: “PRIME SHOT” in a compact uppercase lockup above “CREATIVE” in smaller mono lettering. In the interface, the mark appears as a square olive stamp with the P symbol; the full wordmark is reserved for the hero and footer.

### Signature Brand Color
**Prime Olive — #3F4C2E**. It is grounded, uncommon, and ownable: a field-note green that bridges studio craft, film, and modern commercial work without becoming decorative.

## Style Decisions
- Keep the interface primarily black, warm white, and Prime Olive; do not introduce colorful gradients.
- Use real photographic imagery as the dominant visual element, with text-safe dark overlays where needed.
- Prefer asymmetric editorial composition over a uniform card grid.
- Avoid excessive rounded corners, heavy shadows, and generic SaaS patterns.
- Use Space Grotesk + DM Mono instead of the template’s default font pairing.

## Style Decisions — Review Amendments

- Prime Olive stays a signal color: strongest on indices, arrows, active states, stamps, and italic headline emphasis; broad olive fields are reserved for deliberate accents.
- Informational sections use catalog cues—mono numbering, hairline rules, terse labels, and restrained copy—rather than conventional agency service cards.
- The footer repeats the custom Prime Shot Creative lockup so the identity system is present at both entry and exit points.

## Style Decisions — Current Revision

- Use the supplied geometric P primarily as a compact Prime Olive archive stamp; reserve horizontal lockups for full identity moments only.
- Treat client logos as archival artifacts using controlled off-white, black, olive, and muted print tones rather than pastel card colors.
- Keep brands copy concise, concrete, and observational; avoid sales-led phrases and generic agency claims.

## Style Decisions — Gallery Pages, First-Draft Restoration

- Category pages follow the original Selected Work treatment: a simple left-aligned section label, an oversized title, short explanatory copy, and the established responsive image grid.
- Separate URLs serve only as a clean way to organize the three requested categories; they do not introduce a second visual system or a new archive landing experience.
- Model Shots and Social Media Designs keep their random image order and featured first tile. Behind the Scenes remains a modest text-only no-image section until real BTS photos are supplied.

## Style Decisions — Interaction Revision

- Gallery frames reveal their index, a concise viewing cue, and a Prime Olive signal on hover or keyboard focus; this makes each image read as a cataloged contact-sheet artifact without obscuring the photography.
- Headline movement is limited to a small, alternating horizontal scroll drift; it supports the cinematic editorial rhythm without turning the page into a kinetic display.
- Every gallery frame retains a visible mono index and Prime Olive rule; client-color-heavy imagery is therefore held inside the studio’s dark archive system even before interaction.
